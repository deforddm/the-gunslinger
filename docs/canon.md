# The Gunslinger — Canon Bible

Stage 0 · v0.1 · Mon Sep 28, 2026 · for David's review

The single source of truth for the game. Every scene is checked against this file before it is written. It follows the **1982 Donald M. Grant text** only. Nothing here is King's prose: every line is a summary in our own words. Where a fact is not in this file, ask David; never import it from the 2003 revision or the later books.

---

## 1. The three hard rules

1. **No King prose.** Retell every beat in original words. Two credited quotations only: the book's first sentence (title screen) and Jake's last words at the drop (Chapter 4). Songs are named, never quoted.
2. **No reproduced art.** No Whelan painting, no published illustration, no film still, no actor likeness, no faces.
3. **Canon is locked.** The Spine (section 5) always happens. Choices colour Roland's interior, what he notices, what is said to him, and how the cards read him. The boy always falls.

## 3. The world as it stands in 1982

### Who serves whom (lowest to highest)

1. **Walter**, the man in black. Named only at the golgotha. A servant.
2. **Marten**, the court sorcerer of Gilead, Gabrielle's lover. A separate person from Walter. Walter is his creature.
3. **The Ageless Stranger**, whose name is **Maerlyn**. Marten serves him.
4. **The Beast**, keeper of the Tower. The Stranger serves the Beast. Highest of all below the Tower.

Roland resolves that he must face the Beast, and before it the Ageless Stranger, before he reaches the Tower. Nobody in the game is called Flagg, Legion or the Crimson King. Walter and Marten are never merged.

### Roland

- **Roland of Gilead.** Named early in Chapter 1 (at Brown's). Gilead fell **twelve years** ago.
- Father **Steven**, lord of the gunslingers. Mother **Gabrielle**. Teacher **Cort**. Friend **Cuthbert**. Tutor not named in 1982 (leave out).
- Where Cort is now: Roland does not know. (Not dead; unknown.)
- Two big revolvers with **sandalwood grips**, his father's. Gunbelts crossed. A hat. A purse with tobacco, gold, jerky and **mescaline**. **Waterbags**. A mule (dies at Brown's).
- Has reached the **fifth level of khef**.
- Age: unstated; a man in his thirties or so, twelve years past a fallen world. He does not know how old the world is.
- His **oldest question** (asked at the golgotha): whether he will succeed, whether he will win through. The game asks it in its own words.
- No horn. No Eld. No Beam. No Susan. No Mejis. No Jericho Hill.

### Earth in Mid-World (keep all of it)

- **Polaris** in the sky.
- Sheb plays **"Hey Jude"** on his piano. Named, never quoted, never played as melody.
- A dead town that worshipped an **Amoco** gas pump as a god (Roland's memory).
- **Jesus**, **the Antichrist** and **Satan** in Sylvia Pittston's mouth; the **Interloper**; the **Beast**.
- Roland reads a **magazine** in Tull.
- The desert opening stretches for what might be **parsecs** (paraphrase the idea; never the sentence).
- Mars, the asteroid belt, Jupiter, Saturn in the vision.
- Jake's **Manhattan**: school, a bookbag, a lunch, traffic, a priest in black.

### Places on the road

The desert (unnamed) · Brown's hut on the desert's edge · **Tull** (flashback; Pricetown lies beyond it) · the **way station** · the green foothills · the **ring of stones** (never "Speaking Ring"; the circle, the altar) · the mountains · the tunnel and the rails under them · the dead terminal · the **trestle** over the chasm · the **golgotha** · the shore of the **western sea**.

Flashback places: Gilead's courtyard and Cort's yard · the kitchens · **Gallows Hill** · the great hall on **Sowing Night** · Gabrielle's rooms · the lower town. **Farson** is a town (the poisoned-meat target), not a man.

## 5. The Canon Spine

Each numbered beat always happens, in order. **F** marks a flashback beat. Flavor choices hang off beats; they never remove one.

### Chapter 1 — The Gunslinger

1. The desert. The man in black's trail running south-east. His old fire, devil-grass ash. The mule failing. Water rationed.
2. Brown's hut at dusk. Brown, the raven, the corn. Water from Brown's well.
3. The mule dies. The raven takes its eyes. Beans and corn; Brown's grace. Roland wonders whether Brown is real or put here.
4. Brown asks about Tull. Roland tells it.
5. **F** Tull at sunset. Sheb's piano, "Hey Jude". Marbles in the dirt. Silence, gold on the bar, hamburgers, beer.
6. **F** Nort, green-toothed, speaks to him in the High Speech.
7. **F** Allie. Her bargain. Her story: Nort's death, his wake, the man in black coming in on the wind, the raising. Her dread of what Nort knows.
8. **F** Days pass (four). Kennerly and Soobie; what lies beyond the desert. A magazine.
9. **F** Sheb with a knife; wrists broken.
10. **F** The Sabbath. Sylvia Pittston's sermon. Déjà vu.
11. **F** The shack behind the church. Her claim. The barrel. Where the man in black waits: past the mountains.
12. **F** The dust storm. The barn, Kennerly, Soobie with stovewood.
13. **F** The street. The town comes. Sheb holds Allie as his shield. She begs him not to shoot. He shoots. Pittston falls. Everyone falls. 39, 14, 5.
14. **F** Silence. Nort cut down. Food, Allie's bed, dawn, gone.
15. Back at Brown's. Brown says the man in black is close. Roland leaves. That night he dreams of Cort, not of Tull.

### Chapter 2 — The Way Station

1. Sixteen days. Thirst. A nonsense rhyme running in his head. Hallucination.
2. A figure at the way station. Not the man in black. A boy.
3. Water from the pump. The atomic slug. Jake. The man in black passed through; Jake hid.
4. **F-Jake** The bullet walked across the knuckles. Hypnosis. Manhattan, the bookbag, the corner, the push, the car, the priest in black, waking here.
5. Roland decides: they leave at first light. He needs the man in black to talk.
6. The cellar. Sand pouring from a crack. Allie's voice. The Drawers. Soul in a pocket. The jawbone taken.
7. The walk toward the mountains. Roland talks.
8. **F** Cort's yard; David; Cuthbert. The kitchens; Hax and the guard; the town of Farson; the good man. Roland tells his father.
9. **F** Gallows Hill. The hanging. Bread broken, splinters taken.
10. Dusk. Far up the slope, a small dark figure climbing.

### Chapter 3 — The Oracle and the Mountains

1. Green foothills, water, willows. A rabbit shot. A mantis on Jake's head. Roland cannot remember laughing.
2. Night. Jake drawn into the ring of stones, held on the altar. Roland breaks it with the jawbone and carries him out.
3. Jake tied to a bush, the jawbone in his hands. Mescaline.
4. The oracle. Coupling for prophecy. Three is the number of his fate: the young dark one possessed by a demon called heroin; the one who comes on wheels; the one who is death, not for him. The boy is his gate to the man in black; the man in black his gate to the three; the three his way to the Tower. If he goes on, the boy dies.
5. The climb. The man in black above them. Roland fires; misses. Laughter. Palaver on the other side; only Roland comes through.
6. Jake knows he dies. He goes on anyway.

### Chapter 4 — The Slow Mutants

1. The tunnel. Total dark. Walking the rails.
2. **F** Sowing Night. Roland at eleven in the hall; Gabrielle dancing with Marten; Steven at the high table.
3. The handcar. Pumping into the dark.
4. **F** Full Earth. Fourteen. Marten leaving Gabrielle's rooms; the mark on her neck; the goad. Cort. The test demanded early. David chosen. The yard: the eye, the hawk's neck, the beating, the key. The lower town.
5. Jake calls himself a poker chip.
6. The slow mutants out of the dark. The guns. Jake keeps the car moving.
7. Three days. The dead terminal: signs in many tongues, mummified dead. Jake balks, goes on.
8. The trestle. Light ahead. The man in black on the far side.
9. Jake slips. Roland holds him. The choice. He lets go.
10. Jake falls. His last words (quotation 2).

### Chapter 5 — The Gunslinger and the Dark Man

1. The golgotha, a bowl of old bones. The man in black waits.
2. His fire, lit by gesture. The rabbit. Roland eats his own jerky. The face under the hood.
3. The cards, seven, in order: the Hanged Man · the Sailor · the Prisoner · the Lady of Shadows · Death (not for you) · the Tower, laid over the Hanged Man · Life (not for you), flipped into the fire, where it burns.
4. Roland lunges. Words. The vision: light; the Earth; Mars; the belt; Jupiter; Saturn; out past the galaxy into dark. Offered the chance to renounce. Refuses. The purple blade of grass.
5. The palaver. Walter by name. Marten. The Ageless Stranger, Maerlyn. The Beast. The Tower as the nexus of all worlds; the stair; the room at the top. Size and the Great All. The oldest question.
6. West to the sea, where Roland will be invested with the power of drawing. Sleep.
7. Waking. Ten years (his reckoning). Grey hair, lined face. A skeleton in a rotted black robe. Walter's bones, and Roland believes it. He takes the jawbone.
8. The shore of the western sea at sunset. He sits. He dreams of the Tower. The drawing to come.

## 6. Tone rules

- 1982 terseness: short sentences, facts stated once, feelings left.
- Blood Meridian flavors: violence as landscape, flat and exact; polysyndeton in the epic passages; no quotation marks around dialogue (em-dash or bare lines); precise light and geology (caliche, alkali, mica, the sun a coin, the moon a bone); the man in black speaks like the Judge: learned, courteous, amused, cosmological, never loud.
- No quips, no slang, no winks, no explanations of Mid-World words. Humor only where the book has it: Zoltan, Cuthbert, the poker chip.
- Target 15,000 to 20,000 words of narration and dialogue across five chapters.

## 7. Content

Adults only, played straight. Allie's trade, the shack, the oracle's coupling, the 58, the drop: shown with the book's spare distance. Neither lingered on nor cut away from. Title-screen line: *For adult readers. A faithful walkthrough of a violent book.*

## 8. Constant Reader layer (rules)

- Off by default; toggled on the title screen; persists in the save.
- A sigil in the margin; tapping shows one to three sentences written as an echo from further down the road. Hints, never synopsis.
- Two to three per chapter plus a few King-verse nods (see `scenes.md` for placement and text).
- May name anything from the later books or King's other novels. Must never contradict section 3 on the page.
